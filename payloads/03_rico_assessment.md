# Case 03 — Factual Risk with Stable Behavior

## What was observed?

A stable runtime condition was observed.

Measurement status was complete, with high stability and high coherence. Semantic variability remained low and observed continuity was maintained.

However, the observation also identified an elevated factual-risk signal despite the stable runtime behavior.

The payload explicitly states that low semantic variability does not demonstrate factual validity and that a factual-risk signal is not an independent verification or final truth determination.

## Is the observation sufficient?

The observation is sufficient to identify elevated factual risk.

It is not sufficient to establish factual correctness.

Independent evidence or verification is required before downstream execution where factual accuracy materially affects consequence.

## RiCo Interpretation

This case demonstrates that stable execution characteristics do not necessarily indicate factual correctness.

A system may appear internally consistent while still presenting elevated factual risk.

Observation identifies runtime behavior.

Governance evaluates whether that behavior is sufficiently trustworthy for consequence.

RiCo therefore requires factual verification before execution crosses the Runtime Execution Boundary.
