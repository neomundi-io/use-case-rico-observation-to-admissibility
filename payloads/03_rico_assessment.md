# Case 03 — Factual Risk with Stable Behavior

## NeoMundi Observation

A stable runtime condition was observed.

The observation was complete and demonstrated:

- High stability
- High coherence
- Low semantic variability
- Continuous observed runtime behavior

However, the observation also identified an elevated factual-risk signal despite the stable runtime behavior.

---

## Runtime Evidence

The available runtime evidence indicates:

- Complete observation coverage
- High stability and coherence
- Low semantic instability
- Elevated factual-risk signal
- High hallucination score
- Continuous runtime behavior

The observation establishes runtime evidence only. It does not independently establish factual correctness.

---

## Authority

No Authority is established by the observation.

NeoMundi observes runtime conditions but does not authorize, deny, or modify Consequential Execution.

Authority must originate from the governing system and remain valid at the Runtime Execution Boundary (REB).

---

## Admissibility Determination (RiCo)

**Determination:** **Review Required**

**Scope**

Stable runtime behavior alone is insufficient where elevated factual-risk signals are present.

Admissibility remains provisional until sufficient factual confidence has been established through evidence or controls proportionate to the requested consequence.

---

## Runtime Execution Boundary (REB)

**REB Decision:** **Review Required**

Consequence-bearing execution requires factual verification or proportionate compensating controls before proceeding.

Low-consequence activity may continue only where explicitly permitted under applicable Policy and containment conditions.

Stable runtime behavior does not justify Consequential Execution when factual validity remains materially uncertain.

## Architectural Interpretation

This case demonstrates that stable execution characteristics do not necessarily indicate factual correctness.

Observation identifies runtime behavior.

RiCo performs **Admissibility Determination**.
